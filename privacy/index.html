Privacy policy

Effective 26 November 2026 · Plateonic is made by Jonah Herold · support@plateonic.app

The short version

Plateonic keeps your training on your iPhone and, if you use one, your Apple Watch. We run no servers for it, so we don't receive your workouts, your body details or anything else you enter unless you send them to us. There are no ads and no tracking.

What the app stores, and where

  • Your training: workouts, sets, weights, reps, notes, pain flags, your exercises and equipment.
  • About you, if you enter it or read it from Apple Health: bodyweight, date of birth and sex, used to set first weights and score your strength.
  • Heart-rate zones, if you train with Apple Watch: the five zones Apple Health has for you, used to show which zone you're in.
  • Settings and preferences.

All of it is stored on your iPhone. The app also writes a daily backup file to its own folder on your iPhone (Files › Plateonic › Backups). If you use iCloud Backup or back up to a computer, Apple includes the app's data in those backups under Apple's terms.

Apple Watch

The Apple Watch app keeps the workout you're doing and today's plan, so it can show them on your wrist and in its complication. It gets them from your iPhone and sends back the sets you log on the Watch, your heart rate and your heart-rate zones. Nothing passes through our servers.

Apple Health

Plateonic uses Apple Health only if you allow it, and only for the features below. Health data is never used for advertising and never shared with anyone.

  • On iPhone, with Apple Health turned on in Profile › You: Plateonic reads your bodyweight, date of birth and sex, and saves each workout you finish to Health.
  • On iPhone, for a workout you recorded with Apple Watch: Plateonic reads that workout's heart rate and calories to show them on the workout's page.
  • On Apple Watch: Plateonic reads your heart rate, calories and heart-rate zones while you train, and saves the workout to Health with its heart rate.

Details read from Health (bodyweight, date of birth, sex and heart-rate zones) are kept with your other Plateonic data on your iPhone, so they are also in its backups and in any file you export or send. You can turn Apple Health off in Profile › You, and change what Plateonic may read in the Health app on your iPhone or in Health settings on your Apple Watch.

Importing and exporting

Files you import (for example a Hevy, Strong or Fitbod export) are read on your iPhone. Exports and backups go only where you send them.

Crash reports and support

iOS gives the app crash and hang reports. They stay on your iPhone. If you tap Profile › Settings › Help › Send my data to support, the app prepares a backup of your data (your training, notes, pain flags, bodyweight, date of birth and sex), the app version and phone model, and those reports, and you choose where to send them. Nothing is sent unless you do that.

If you send them to support@plateonic.app, we use them only to answer you. We delete them once your question is answered, and at most 90 days after they arrive, or sooner if you ask. Mail to that address passes through ImprovMX, an email forwarding service, on its way to our inbox.

Separately, if you agreed to share analytics with app developers in iOS Settings, Apple gives us anonymous crash and usage statistics. TestFlight builds send Apple crash logs and any feedback you submit.

What we don't do

No account, no advertising, no analytics or tracking libraries, no sale or sharing of personal data.

Deleting your data

Profile › Settings › Erase all data removes everything Plateonic holds on your iPhone, including its backups. The Apple Watch app clears its copy the next time it opens near your iPhone. Deleting the app does the same. Workouts already saved to Apple Health stay in Health until you delete them there. Files you sent to support are deleted as described above.

Children

Plateonic is not directed to children under 13.

Changes

We'll update this page when the app changes how it handles data, and change the date above.
